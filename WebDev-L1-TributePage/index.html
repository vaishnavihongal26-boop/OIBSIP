HER STORY

A mind driven by curiosity.

Rosalind Franklin was born in London in 1920 and developed a strong interest in science from an early age. She studied natural sciences at Newnham College, Cambridge, where she built a strong foundation in chemistry and physical science.

Franklin later worked in Paris, where she became highly skilled in X-ray crystallography. This technique allowed scientists to study the structure of materials at a very small scale.

In 1951, she joined King's College London and began studying DNA. Her precise X-ray diffraction experiments produced important evidence about the molecule's structure.

Her famous Photograph 51 showed a clear pattern associated with a helical structure. Franklin later continued her research on viruses at Birkbeck College until her death in 1958 at the age of 37.

PHOTOGRAPH 51

An image that changed science.

In 1952, Rosalind Franklin and her team produced Photograph 51, an X-ray diffraction image of DNA.

The image provided important evidence that DNA had a helical structure.
